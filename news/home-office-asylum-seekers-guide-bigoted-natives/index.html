Home Office Hands Asylum Seekers Guide To Reassure Them About Hostile, Bigoted Natives Who Wrongly Suspect They May Need Explaining Britain’s Sexual And Domestic-Abuse Norms

23 August 2026 · 3 min

LONDON — The Home Office has distributed behavioural guidance to asylum seekers explaining that the white native population has the false belief that they are a people with a unique claim to the country and that this belief must be challenged as part of Britain’s ongoing commitment to inclusion, diversity and the eradication of indigenous prejudice.

While hate speech and hate crime laws are in effect to protect people of BAME minority backgrounds, the guidance reportedly warns that white Britons may express discomfort when told that their historical culture, institutions and national identity are merely one perspective among many.

Officials said the material was intended to reassure asylum seekers that any hostility they encounter from native residents is likely to reflect insecurity, ignorance or latent bigotry rather than a legitimate concern about social cohesion and should be reported to the police and the nearest approved community cohesion officer.

“New arrivals should understand that Britain belongs equally to everyone,” said Home Office inclusion adviser Priya Chattopadhyay. “Whether they have been here for generations or have arrived recently and have urgent needs to fulfil.”

The guidance reportedly explains that native Britons must not assume that newcomers should adapt to British customs, because such expectations may reproduce colonial hierarchies.

At the same time, asylum seekers are encouraged to familiarise themselves with British law, public services, safeguarding procedures and acceptable workplace conduct.

The document reportedly warns asylum seekers that some white Britons may complain that their communities are changing too quickly.

Such complaints, it says, should be understood as manifestations of racial anxiety and should not be treated as evidence that the pace or scale of change might be politically relevant.

The guidance also advises newcomers that references to national identity, inherited customs or demographic continuity may be attempts to exclude minorities from public life.

Critics said this appeared to define racism as any preference for the continuity of the majority culture while defining diversity as the rapid transformation of that culture.

The Home Office rejected the characterisation.

“Diversity is not about replacing anyone,” a spokesperson said.

“What happens if the majority becomes a minority?”

“That is a hypothetical. But it would be a good thing, because it would demonstrate that Britain had finally overcome the exclusionary assumptions built into majority status.”

Asked what happens when the newcomers act in a way that mirrored the explicit values, culture, behaviour theological directives, or tribal dynamics of the population of the region they left behind because it was a hellscape due to said explicit values, culture, behaviour theological directives, or tribal dynamics, a spokesperson asked the person who asked the question to remain seated and called the police who arrested the journalist for “incitement to community tension”.

At present time the journalist was charged with 28 offences, including incitement to community tension, aggravated questioning, possession of an unauthorised demographic concern and attempting to establish a causal relationship between immigration and social outcomes.

The Home Office said the arrest demonstrated that Britain remained committed to free speech, provided that nobody used it to say anything liable to make anyone feel judged, excluded, stereotyped, offended, alarmed, distressed or insufficiently celebrated.
