Revised Time Estimates Reveal You Won’t Be Long Gone When World Goes To Absolute Shit

5 August 2026 · 3 min

CANBERRA — Australians have been reassured that they will not, as previously hoped, be safely dead by the time the world deteriorates into an unliveable administrative slaughterhouse, according to revised government time estimates released today.

The updated modelling found that most adults currently aged between 35 and 60 would remain alive, economically active and fully responsible for adapting when housing, energy, public services, climate, social trust and basic institutional competence complete their transition from “under pressure” to “absolutely fucking rooted”.

Previous estimates had allowed citizens to believe the worst consequences would occur sometime after their deaths, possibly affecting children they had declined to have or future generations they had never met.

That assumption has now been corrected.

“You won’t be long gone,” said lead modeller Dr Jayne Richards.

“You will, in fact, be here.”

“You will most likely be older, poorer, less mobile and waiting eleven hours for an ambulance, but you will absolutely be present for what’s to come.”

Richards said improved forecasting had moved the expected collapse window forward from the comfortably abstract later this century to the more actionable during your finite life span.

“This is excellent news for planning,” she said.

“People can no longer dismiss civilisational decline as somebody else’s problem. They can now prepare to experience it personally.”

“Well, when I say ’they can prepare’… I mean they can prepare in the same way you can prepare for a house fire by purchasing a very detailed brochure explaining what smoke is, while the house continues to actively burn around you.

The revised timetable incorporates accelerating housing unaffordability, infrastructure failure, mass population growth, ethnic and religious fragmentation, energy instability, war, declining institutional legitimacy, persistent inflation and the government’s proven ability to respond to every worsening condition by creating a portal.

Treasury welcomed the findings.

“The important message is that people should not panic,” a spokesperson said.

“There won’t be a single big event. These changes will occur gradually, allowing households sufficient time to blame themselves.”

The spokesperson said Australians would be supported through the transition with targeted rebates, awareness campaigns and an online calculator showing which organs could be sold without immediately affecting workforce participation.

Citizens had previously consoled themselves with the belief that they would not live to see the full consequences of decisions currently described as necessary, temporary, carefully managed or broadly consistent with expert advice.

The new report says this optimism was misplaced.

“You will see the shit hit the fan,” Richards said.

“You will see the fan replaced by a cheaper contractor.”

“You will then be asked to submit feedback about the resulting shit-delivery experience.”

The government insisted that the report did not predict collapse.

It merely modelled a prolonged period in which everything became more expensive, less reliable, more surveiled and harder to refuse, while officials continued describing the country as resilient.

“Collapse implies an end-state. This is simply ongoing service degradation.”

Tom Frampton, 42, said he had assumed he would die before Australia became an overcrowded, unaffordable biometric holding pen connected to several malfunctioning digital services.

“I thought there’d be a transition period,” he said. “Maybe I’d cark it before it all goes proper bad.”

Frampton said the discovery had altered his retirement planning.

“I used to save because I wanted freedom,” he said.

“Now I’m saving because apparently freedom will cost $6,000 a month.”

The report concludes that today’s adults can expect to remain alive through the abandonment of nearly every assumption on which they organised their lives and they will not be “long gone”.
