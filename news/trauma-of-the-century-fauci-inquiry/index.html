Trauma Of The Century Resurfaces After Fauci Inquiry

3 August 2026 · 5 min

MELBOURNE — A local man who spent four years demanding that the defining trauma of his adult life be dragged back out of the memory hole has brought up harrowing memories for the first time in years, regarding state tyrannisation and the knowledge that his former rulers were still at large when American former Chief Medical Advisor Anthony Fauci appeared in the US Senate and refused to answer most questions.

DevOps Engineer Tom Frampton, 42, had repeatedly complained that the pandemic, lockdowns, mandates, border closures, business destruction and compulsory public-health theatre had been quietly filed away in February 2022.

Frampton, who spent extended periods confined to his Melbourne home because of decisions made by a premier who was definitely not dictatorial, said he had hoped Anthony Fauci’s appearance before the US Senate might finally expose what happened, who knew what and whether anyone in authority had lied.

Then, he hoped, this would cascade to other countries, heads would roll, and all Western leaders responsible for it would finally face consequences for house-arresting, fining, prosecuting, locking down, mandating, coercing, censoring, and otherwise restricting movement and livelihoods.

“If I, in my own job, refused to deploy releases sent to me, mangled the DNS, demanded money for every Sev 1 incident, or held production hostage until I got a promotion, and I told my head of IT, ‘I did it just because, and you can all rim me hard’, I’d be out on my arse.”

“But if I wore a suit or a North Face jacket and shook hands with Prime Minsters, donors, and one day decided to command the whereabouts of the entire population during a public health emergency, I’d get the support of most of the media, a fat retirement package, a book deal and speech gigs.”

He watched the hearing online.

“The closest we’ve had to any of our betters seeing the inside of a prison was this one,” Frampton said.

“An old man appeared before the American Senate and said, ‘Yeah, get fucked. I ain’t tellin’ you nuffin’.’”

Fauci repeatedly invoked the Fifth Amendment when questioned about the origins of COVID-19, American funding of coronavirus research and discrepancies between his public statements and private records.

Frampton initially leaned towards the laptop with the alert posture of a man expecting the machinery of justice to start moving.

It did not.

“No one’s seat started to creak because of this,” he said.

“No marshal walked over. No handcuffs appeared. Nobody said, ‘Righto, mate, you’ve had five years to get your story straight.’”

“They just kept asking questions and he kept declining to answer them.”

After several hours, Frampton realised the hearing was not the beginning of accountability but accountability-themed content.

“It’s one step forward, two steps back,” Frampton said. “The Yanks finally get him under oath, and the oath apparently gives him the right to say nothing.”

Legal experts explained that invoking the Fifth Amendment was not an admission of guilt and protected witnesses from being compelled to incriminate themselves.

Frampton said he understood the constitutional principle but remained disappointed by its production values.

“You spend years being told that only conspiracy theorists have questions,” he said.

“Then the man with all the answers arrives and exercises his legal right not to answer.”

The hearing followed the release of Fauci’s pandemic-era journal entries and renewed scrutiny over what officials privately considered plausible about the virus’s origins.

For Frampton, the documents represented another promising instalment in a series that had never reached a conclusion.

“We get emails, diaries, redactions and testimony,” he said. “Every six months, someone reveals that the people who said the lab theory was impossible were privately discussing the lab theory.”

“Then a fact-checker explains that this does not prove the exact thing nobody is permitted to investigate properly.”

The process has left Frampton increasingly unsure what accountability would even look like.

“I don’t need Nuremberg,” he said. “Well, I do, but what do I know, I’m just a rank-and-file cunt who has no meaningful influence to do anything.”

Frampton said the pandemic had been transformed from an event into a disagreement about tone.

Nobody had ordered healthy citizens indoors; difficult decisions had been made.

Nobody had coerced medical treatment; communities had been protected.

Nobody had suspended ordinary life indefinitely; temporary measures had been extended in response to evolving conditions.

Nobody had censored dissent; platforms had reduced harmful misinformation in consultation with government stakeholders.

“And nobody was responsible,” Frampton said.

“That’s the important bit.”

He said the Russian invasion of Ukraine provided the perfect emergency exit.

“Funny how Putin’s invasion ended the pandemic, opened the floodgates for new arrivals that would previously be barred from entry or had to spend two weeks in quarantine hotels, before suddenly being waved through as students or skilled workers.”

Frampton was especially infuriated by friends, family and colleagues who regarded the pandemic as “yeah, that was a weird couple of years”.

“It would be weird if you were stuck in an unusually long airport delay,” he said. “It would be weird if your office Christmas party ended with the CFO asleep in a fountain.”

“It is not ‘weird’ when the government confines millions of people to their homes, closes businesses by decree, limits how far they can travel, decides which relationships count as essential and sends police to question people sitting alone on park benches.”

“That’s not quirky. That’s not an odd little cultural moment we all lived through together. That’s the state discovering it can put the population under house arrest and have half the country demand stricter enforcement.”

At publishing time, Fauci had returned home after the hearing and was reportedly not in custody, while Frampton was still refreshing the livestream in case accountability began at any moment.
