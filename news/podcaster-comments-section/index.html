Podcaster’s Comment Section Achieves Full Strategic Readiness Following Another Video Telling Viewers The West Is All But Finished

26 July 2026 · 4 min

The comment section beneath doom podcaster Julian May’s latest upload has reportedly achieved full strategic readiness after hundreds of viewers concluded that the West was finished, elections were decorative and the only remaining question was whether they should buy resistance bands before or after forming a parallel government.

May, host of Mayday, had uploaded a 54-minute video titled “THE FINAL PEACEFUL WINDOW IS CLOSING FASTER THAN WE THOUGHT”, in which he urged viewers to remain calm, vote strategically and prepare for several outcomes he repeatedly stressed could not be discussed on the platform.

Within minutes, the comments had developed into a self-sustaining civilisational psychosis ward populated by men informing one another that nobody was coming, everything was deliberate and the time for ordinary politics had ended at some unspecified point between the last upload and breakfast.

“If elections worked, we wouldn’t be in this position,” wrote one viewer beneath the video, where May reminded viewers to vote harder.

Another said he had once been terrified by the prospect of civil war but was now terrified by the prospect of not fighting one, a psychological development greeted by 614 likes, several saluting emojis and one reply asking whether he had started training legs.

A third commenter announced that “we took the wrong side in the Second World War”, before being corrected by another user who said the real mistake had been allowing women’s university education to continue afterwards.

Several viewers urged men to train in hand-to-hand combat and weapons handling, despite follow-up replies revealing that their current routines consisted mainly of watching instructional videos, bench-pressing intermittently and firing air rifles at soft-drink cans on rural property owned by somebody else.

“Things are looking less peaceful by the day,” one commenter wrote. “We need to form parallel societies and prepare.”

The user later clarified that his proposed parallel society would initially operate through a Telegram group, a monthly Zoom call and discounted access to an online masculinity course.

Others appeared satisfied that all existing events formed part of a single coordinated plan.

“It’s all deliberate, and it’s going swimmingly,” one viewer wrote beneath a discussion of housing shortages, fertility decline, migration, energy prices, council waste collection and the disappearance of his preferred flavour of protein yoghurt.

Another posted only K A L E R G I, prompting dozens of replies from users who appeared relieved that somebody had finally identified the surname responsible for everything.

“No one is coming. Remember that,” wrote a man whose profile biography contained links to six podcasters, three political parties and an emergency-preparedness company offering free delivery.

“There’s only one solution. One,” wrote another viewer, declining to specify the solution or respond to replies, which all guessed something different.

One commenter warned that “the carbon they want to reduce is what you exhale”, a claim that received enthusiastic support from users who nevertheless remained subscribed to smart electricity plans, loyalty programmes, cloud photo storage and vehicles capable of transmitting diagnostic data to the manufacturer.

Another said a full-scale Forever Purge would represent a drastic improvement over conditions in his homeland, though his posting history indicated that he currently lived in a detached house in regional Queensland and had recently complained that the local council failed to mow a nature strip.

Several users had informed their wives that the garage would now serve as a logistics hub, shortly before being asked to move several boxes of Christmas decorations.

One user announced that he had withdrawn his consent from the system, then asked whether anyone knew how to dispute a parking fine online.

May’s moderators removed several comments containing explicit threats, leaving behind hundreds that merely described violence as inevitable, morally understandable, historically validated and likely to be initiated by people other than the commenter.

“This channel does not endorse unlawful activity,” read a pinned moderation notice above a thread debating ammunition storage, physical fitness and which historical collapse offered the most useful tactical lessons.

May himself entered the discussion only once, giving a heart to a comment reading, “Peaceful men can only be pushed so far,” before replying: “Stay calm. Build capacity.”

The phrase was immediately interpreted as an instruction to lift weights, start families, purchase radios, form neighbourhood patrols, create alternative currencies, study irregular warfare and upgrade to May’s paid tier.
