REPORT: At Every Timeline Branch During Wuhan Virus Crisis, Authorities Chose Worst Available Option

15 August 2026 · 9 min

GENEVA — Temporal researchers have concluded that at nearly every major decision point during the Wuhan Pandemic, governments across the Western world somehow selected the branch producing the greatest combination of social damage, economic destruction, institutional distrust and subsequent refusal to admit anything had gone wrong.

The findings come from a three-year study by the Institute for Applied Temporal Mechanics, whose researchers analysed hundreds of thousands of parallel universes created by individual policy decisions made between 2020 and 2022.

“We initially assumed hindsight was distorting public perception,” said lead temporal physicist Dr Carla Pickering.

“So we built a system capable of observing alternate timelines.”

“That made things much worse.”

Researchers identified more than 340,000 significant decision branches covering border policy, lockdowns, schools, hospitals, vaccination, policing, public communication, censorship, fiscal policy and the eventual process of pretending none of it had been particularly remarkable.

In nearly every case, our own timeline ranked among the worst available outcomes.

“Statistically, this should be almost impossible,” Pickering said. “You would expect governments occasionally to stumble into the second or third-worst decision.”

The first major branch occurred when authorities were deciding whether to take the emerging virus seriously before widespread transmission.

Timeline A imposed targeted border controls early.

Timeline B expanded hospital preparedness and stockpiled medical equipment.

Timeline C pursued aggressive testing while maintaining ordinary life as long as possible.

The Prime Timeline selected:

Call border restrictions racist, maintain international movement, then impose unprecedented domestic movement restrictions several weeks later.

A second junction occurred when officials realised the virus posed highly unequal risks across age groups.

Alternative timelines focused resources heavily on elderly and medically vulnerable populations.

Others permitted younger and healthier people considerably greater freedom while building hospital capacity.

The Prime Timeline instead selected:

Treat entire population as approximately interchangeable risk units and prohibit children from using playground equipment. Prohibit access to all parks and all outdoor recreation areas entirely.

A temporal researcher reportedly stared at the output for several minutes.

“Why the playgrounds and parks?”

Pickering said nobody had found a satisfactory answer.

Further branches showed similar patterns.

When deciding how long emergency powers should remain in force, alternate universes selected clearly defined limits, parliamentary scrutiny or automatic expiry mechanisms.

The Prime Timeline selected:

Just keep extending them.

When considering whether people should be permitted outside alone:

No.

Whether beaches should remain accessible:

Probably not.

Whether police should question people sitting individually on park benches:

Yes.

Whether dying relatives should be allowed visitors:

Let’s not get sentimental.

Whether people should be allowed to cross state borders to see family:

Depends whether they can prove an appropriately bureaucratic reason.

When rescue dogs attract rescuers who travel to pick them up:

Kill the dogs.

Whether professional athletes and politically useful entrants could receive exemptions unavailable to ordinary citizens:

Unless you’re a professional athlete or a politically useful entrant, sit down and shut up.

The school-closure branch produced particularly striking results.

In thousands of observed timelines, policymakers weighed the comparatively low risk to children against educational loss, developmental damage, family pressure and the burden placed on disadvantaged students.

The Prime Timeline chose prolonged disruption and then appeared surprised several years later when children displayed educational and behavioural problems.

“Other universes actually modelled this beforehand,” Pickering said. “Ours commissioned reports afterwards.”

Economic policy displayed the same strange tendency.

Faced with a government-mandated shutdown of large sections of ordinary economic activity, alternate timelines used tightly targeted support intended to preserve viable businesses and household incomes.

The Prime Timeline produced enormous fiscal stimulus, monetary expansion, supply disruption and years of distorted incentives before policymakers began wondering why everything became expensive.

“The best part is the inflation branch,” temporal economist Martin Reeves said.

“You can actually watch policymakers inject extraordinary amounts of money into an economy whose productive capacity they have deliberately constrained.”

“Then three branches later somebody appears on television saying inflation is mysterious.”

The public-health messaging branches were no better.

Authorities could have communicated uncertainty, acknowledged changing evidence and explained trade-offs honestly.

Instead, the Prime Timeline repeatedly selected:

State current assumption with absolute moral certainty.

Then, when evidence changed:

Adopt opposite position with absolute moral certainty.

Then:

Deny previous position was ever stated.

Then:

Accuse anyone possessing screenshots of spreading misinformation.

“In most universes, contradictory public messaging damages credibility,” said Pickering.

“In ours, authorities responded to declining credibility by demanding greater trust.”

“This produced an entirely new branch we had not previously thought physically possible.”

Researchers were particularly fascinated by the mask timeline.

Multiple alternatives existed, ranging from early precautionary recommendations to context-dependent rules based on transmission risk.

The Prime Timeline moved through several distinct stages: Masks don’t do anything, masks potentially useful, masks mandatory, masks mandatory while walking five metres into a restaurant, masks removable once seated, masks necessary outdoors, masks unnecessary outdoors, masks proof of social responsibility. Questions about mask policy evidence of moral defect.

“This universe had somehow converted a respiratory intervention into ceremonial clothing,” Pickering said.

“It was genuinely impressive.”

Vaccination produced the largest and most unstable branch network.

Alternative timelines preserved informed consent while strongly encouraging vaccination among those at greatest risk.

Others recognised prior infection, permitted individual risk assessment or acknowledged uncertainty about transmission effects.

The Prime Timeline instead discovered that an intervention initially promoted principally for personal protection could become a condition of employment, travel, entry and ordinary social participation.

Researchers observed healthy adults being told they were exercising free choice while employers and governments systematically removed alternatives.

“This branch was labelled VOLUNTARY,” Pickering said.

“We checked whether that was a translation error.”

“It wasn’t.”

One parallel universe required people to make their own decisions.

It survived.

Researchers described the finding as embarrassing.

The censorship branch was similarly unusual.

Authorities faced a surge of dubious claims, conspiracy theories, legitimate scientific disagreement and uncertain emerging evidence.

Possible responses included transparency, open debate and rapid publication of underlying information.

The Prime Timeline selected close cooperation between government bodies, technology platforms and institutional experts to suppress claims judged harmful.

Several suppressed claims subsequently became reasonable subjects for investigation.

Researchers then watched institutions explain that this did not retrospectively validate anybody who had asked about them earlier.

“The timeline developed a fascinating epistemological rule,” Pickering said.

“A claim could be misinformation on Tuesday and a respectable question on Thursday.”

“The person punished on Tuesday received no apology and their name and claim were memory-holed.”

Australia#

Australia generated so many exceptional branches that the temporal computing cluster required additional storage.

Victoria alone produced 2.3 petabytes.

One branch asked whether an entire metropolitan population should be subjected to curfews.

Most universes answered no.

The Prime Timeline asked whether police helicopters and drones were available.

Another branch considered whether citizens should be permitted to travel more than a prescribed distance from home.

The Prime Timeline introduced geographic movement limits and then deployed law enforcement to ensure adults remained within their permitted radius.

An alternate Melbourne never imposed the rule.

Researchers discovered it remained recognisably Melbourne.

“This upset several models,” Pickering said.

A particularly grim branch involved citizens stranded overseas.

Alternate universes allowed nationals to return subject to proportionate quarantine arrangements.

The Prime Timeline severely restricted entry while continuing to describe citizenship as an exceptionally meaningful legal bond.

End Of The Measures#

The Institute also examined the crisis’s conclusion.

Governments had several choices.

They could conduct searching public inquiries.

They could openly evaluate harms caused by specific interventions.

They could identify failures.

They could compensate people unnecessarily harmed.

They could prosecute actual misconduct where appropriate.

Those responsible could be named, investigated, required to explain their decisions in public hearings, banned from further participation in public life, lose their pension and some be put in leg irons and in prison.

They could at least say:

Some of this was wrong.

The Prime Timeline selected:

That Was A Weird Couple Of Years, Weren’t They, Anyway, Ukraine.

Researchers initially rejected the result as impossible.

Yet by 2023, millions of citizens who had spent years living under rules previously only seen in dystopian literature or real-world authoritarian regimes, were being encouraged to remember the period primarily through sourdough, Zoom calls and nostalgic photographs of empty streets.

“The memory-management branch might actually be the most remarkable,” Pickering said.

“You have populations remembering extraordinary state intervention.”

“Then the timeline slowly overlays it with the phrase ‘during COVID’ until nobody can remember who actually made which decision.”

The researchers emphasised that not every decision in the Prime Timeline was necessarily wrong and that governments faced genuine uncertainty, incomplete information and difficult trade-offs.

Their surprise came from the cumulative pattern.

“You’d expect mistakes,” Reeves said.

“You wouldn’t expect someone to hit every fucking pothole.”

Researchers found only one major timeline that ranked consistently worse.

In Timeline 19-K, emergency authorities moved beyond lockdowns into night-time disappearances, newsroom seizures, mass deplatforming, government-issued lists of permissible discussion topics, compulsory injectable tracking technology, public-square exeecutions, mass graves and, during the final containment phase, thermobaric bombardment of designated outbreak zones.

Pickering said the discovery initially provided the team with some comfort.

“For about thirty seconds we thought, well, there you go. We didn’t choose the worst timeline.”

“Turns out ours was the branch immediately before that one.”

But Why?#

The Institute moved quickly to reject the theory that Western leaders in the Prme Timeline are closeted sadists.

“People imagine the authoritarian danger as a room full of sinister men deciding exactly how much freedom to remove,” Pickering said.

“We found mostly politicians staring at polling, advisers modelling which constituency could be pissed off at the lowest electoral cost, public servants minimising departmental exposure and ministers asking whether somebody else could announce it.”

The thermobaric bombardment timeline required leaders prepared to say we are doing this.

The Prime Timeline overwhelmingly preferred:

Advice has been received.
A framework has been developed.
The Chief Health Officer recommends.
Police will operationalise.
Platforms will enforce their own terms of service.
Individual businesses are free to choose, subject to the following mandatory requirements.

Pickering said researchers had initially classified this behaviour as authoritarianism before discovering something more pathetic.

“Authoritarians actually want to rule.”

“These people want to remain in fucking office.”

Researchers found no contemporary Western political leader willing to undertake anything requiring their name to appear beside an irreversible decision.

“No one is Cromwell. No one is Churchill, De Gaulle or Eisenhower. Frankly, no one even wants to be the bloke who cancelled the procurement contract.”

“They don’t build anything. They don’t even write most of the documents carrying their signatures.”

“They manage systems created by people who are long dead, commission consultants to recommend modifications, announce those modifications as reforms and leave before anyone can measure whether they worked.”

The Prime Timeline therefore avoided spectacular tyranny in favour of something researchers considered more culturally authentic: millions of individually deniable acts of cowardice linked together through process.

Nobody ordered society shut. Health advice required it.
Nobody forced anyone to receive treatment. Employers established conditions.
Nobody censored anyone. Platforms independently applied standards after government engagement.
Nobody prevented citizens returning home. Capacity constraints regrettably limited arrivals.
Nobody decided any of it. It just happened.

“The conspiracy theorists actually gave them too much credit,” Pickering said.

At publishing time, the report’s lead researchers had been professionally discredited and removed from several academic bodies, while major outlets variously described them as “pandemic revisionists”, “anti-science contrarians” and “far-right-adjacent temporal misinformation figures”.
